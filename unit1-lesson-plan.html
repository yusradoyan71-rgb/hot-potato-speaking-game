🔥 Warmer — Smartphone Love/Hate
⏱ 8 minutesTeacher → Whole Class
Whole Class ▼
I love my smartphone because … I hate my smartphone because …
Write these two sentences on the board. Tell the class: "I'll give you my example first. I love my smartphone because it keeps me connected to my friends. I hate my smartphone because it distracts me when I'm working." Now — your turn. Think of ONE reason each. You have 30 seconds.

👩‍🏫 What YOU do

  1. Write both sentence starters on the board.
  2. Give your own example out loud.
  3. Give Ss 30 seconds to think silently.
  4. Ask 4–5 students to share. Don't correct language — just build energy.
  5. Ask: "What are the advantages and disadvantages of being connected all the time?"

🎓 What STUDENTS do

  • Think of their own reasons (silent 30s).
  • Share with the class when asked.
  • React to classmates' answers.
😶 If students are shy

Let them write their sentence down first, then read it aloud. Don't cold-call — ask for volunteers first.

⚡ If they finish fast

Ask follow-up: "Do you think smartphones make life better or worse overall? Give 3 reasons."

Say: "Great — you clearly have strong feelings about smartphones! Now let's look at the kinds of videos people watch on social media. Open your books to page 9."

💬 Exercise 3 — Deep Discussion (Main Activity)
⏱ 15 minutesIndividual → Pair → Whole Class
Pair → Class ▼
Read questions 1 to 6 silently. Think about your answers — write 2–3 words for each if you like. You have 1 minute to think, then you'll discuss with your partner.

📝 The 6 questions (p.9)

  1. Which platforms do you use? What for?
  2. How much time do you spend on social media?
  3. Is social media good or bad? Why?
  4. Do you play games online? Is it a good use of time?
  5. What else do you use your phone/computer for?
  6. Do you ever have social-media-free days? How does it feel?

👩‍🏫 Stage sequence

  1. 1 min: Ss read questions, note ideas silently.
  2. 8 min: Pair discussion — both partners answer all questions.
  3. Tell Ss: "Add a reason or example — don't just say yes/no."
  4. 5 min: Whole class — ask each pair to share the most interesting answer.
Useful language: • I use Instagram / TikTok / YouTube for … • I think / I believe social media is … because … • In my opinion, … / Personally, I … • I spend about ___ hours a day on … • I've never tried … / I always …
😶 If students are shy

Tell them: "Just answer 2 questions — whichever ones you feel most comfortable with." Don't force all 6.

⚡ If they finish fast

Extra question: "If you had to give up ONE social media platform forever, which one would it be? Why?"

🐢 If they're slow

Give them questions 1, 3, and 6 only. That's enough for a solid discussion.

😕 If discussion dries up

Read out a question yourself and give your own answer first. This usually restarts the conversation.

Say: "Excellent discussion! Now let's see what the book says about HOW social media works. Next lesson we'll read an article about algorithms."

👩‍🏫 What YOU do

  1. Ask 2–3 students to share something surprising about their partner.
  2. Ask the "algorithm" question — don't explain it yet, just plant curiosity.
  3. Briefly preview Lesson 2: "Next class we read about this — and you'll find the answer."

🎯 Homework suggestion

  • Write 3–5 sentences about your own social media habits.
  • Use: "I usually…", "I sometimes…", "I never…"

🔍 Exercise 2 — Matching (Reading Expert)
⏱ 12 minutesIndividual → Pair check
Individual▼
Read the READING EXPERT box. The strategy is: Read the whole article first to get the main idea. Then read each paragraph and match it to ONE question (A–H). Remember — two questions are NOT needed, so don't worry if two are left over. You have 8 minutes. Work alone.

📋 READING EXPERT Strategy (show on board)

  1. Read the whole article quickly (don't stop at every word).
  2. Read paragraph 1 — what is the main idea?
  3. Find the question (A–H) that matches best.
  4. Repeat for all 6 paragraphs.
  5. Check: 2 questions will be left over.

💡 For weaker classes

You can tell them which two questions are NOT needed: C and G. This reduces pressure and lets them focus on matching.

✅ Answers

1→D   2→B   3→A   4→E   5→H   6→F   (C and G not used)

🎮 Warmer — Team Game with Social Media Verbs
⏱ 8 minutesTeams
Teams▼
Write ALL these verbs on the board (from Exercise 5 on p.11): scroll · click · follow · share · upload · view access · browse · log on · tap · search · download
We're going to play a team game. I'll divide you into teams of 3–4. Each team takes turns — choose a verb from the board and use it in a sentence WITH the correct preposition if needed. If your sentence is correct — 2 points and cross it out. If it's wrong — the OTHER team gets a bonus point if they can correct it. The team with the most points wins!

👩‍🏫 How to run it

  1. Divide into 2–4 teams.
  2. Team 1 chooses a verb → makes a sentence.
  3. You decide if it's correct. If yes: cross out verb, 2 pts.
  4. If wrong: other team can steal with a correction (1 pt).
  5. Continue until all verbs are gone. Highest score wins.

📌 Prepositions (write on board after)

  • search for
  • upload something from your gallery
  • click on
  • tap on
  • log on to
  • scroll through
  • swipe to the right
  • share (something) with
Say: "Excellent! Now we're going to look at grammar — present tenses. You already know these — let's see if you remember the rules."

📘 Grammar Box — Study Together
⏱ 5 minutesWhole Class
Whole Class▼
Open page 12. Read the Grammar box — you have 2 minutes. Then I'll ask you to give me your OWN examples. Don't just read mine.
PRESENT SIMPLE — habits, facts, routines Every morning, I check my phone. She always posts videos on Fridays. PRESENT CONTINUOUS — happening now / around now They're watching a livestream right now. I'm trying to spend less time online. STATE VERBS — NOT used in continuous: know · believe · think · remember · understand want · prefer · hate · like · need hear · seem · taste · own · belong · have

👩‍🏫 What YOU do

  1. Give examples for each tense from YOUR OWN life.
  2. Ask Ss to add their own examples for each section.
  3. Special attention to state verbs — elicit common mistakes: "I'm knowing / I'm wanting".

💡 Teaching Tip

Give extra examples of your daily routine: "Every morning, I get up at 7. I have a shower. At the moment, I'm teaching an English class."

Ask Ss to add their own examples for both tenses before moving on.

🗣️ Exercise 4 — Speaking Guide (Main Task)
⏱ 13 minutesPair Work
Pair Work▼
Now it's your turn. Look at the two new photos in Exercise 4 — one shows students in a classroom with computers, the other shows a teacher with students around a board. Follow the SPEAKING GUIDE. Student A: TALK about the similarities and differences — aim for 30 seconds to 2 minutes! Student B: Listen. When A finishes, ask ONE of the three follow-up questions. Then SWAP roles. You have 8 minutes total.

📋 Speaking Guide — steps

  1. PLAN — both partners: make a quick list of 2–3 similarities and 2–3 differences.
  2. Student A SPEAKS — describes photos using key expressions (aim for ~2 min). Student B TIMES and LISTENS.
  3. Student B ASKS one follow-up question:
    • Which way of learning do you prefer and why?
    • What are the advantages and disadvantages?
    • What other technology do you use to help you learn?
  4. Swap roles. Student B describes the same or different photos.

👩‍🏫 Your role while monitoring

  • Listen for correct use of key expressions.
  • Note common errors (don't interrupt).
  • Encourage students who stop: "Keep going — use a filler phrase!"
  • After: write 2–3 common errors on board and correct them together.
😶 If students are shy

Let them write 3 sentences first, then read them aloud. Even reading is OK — it builds confidence. Gradually reduce the notes they use.

🐢 If they struggle to talk

Give them a "script starter": "Both photos show students learning. In the first photo, we can see… In the second photo, there is…" — just fill in the blanks.

⚡ Fast finishers

Write about the advantages of using technology for learning. Or find a photo of a VR classroom online and compare it to one of the book photos.

😕 Don't know what to say

Encourage them to start with the obvious: "Both photos show people studying." Then push for subtle differences: "In the first photo, there is NO teacher…"

📘 Grammar Box — Gerunds & Infinitives
⏱ 5 minutesWhole Class
Whole Class▼
GERUND (verb + -ing) — use after: avoid, consider, enjoy, imagine, like, prefer, risk, start, stop, suggest e.g. We all enjoy USING apps. Also: after a preposition ("by WALKING") Also: as the subject ("DOING exercise is fun.") INFINITIVE (to + verb) — use after: agree, choose, decide, forget, help, hope, learn, manage, try e.g. You can CHOOSE TO RUN, walk or cycle. Also: after an adjective ("It's easy TO USE.") Also: to say WHY ("to make our lives easier")
Read through the Grammar box. The key point: there is NO rule about WHY some verbs take gerunds and others take infinitives — you simply have to learn them. I'll read the examples aloud. After I read each one, you give me YOUR OWN example with the same verb. Ready?

👩‍🏫 Elicitation technique

  1. Say: "Enjoy + ?" → "USING / PLAYING / WATCHING"
  2. "Hope + ?" → "TO PASS / TO IMPROVE / TO TRAVEL"
  3. "It's easy + ?" → "TO FORGET / TO LEARN / TO LOSE"
  4. Give correction: gerund or infinitive? Ask: "Which verb list is 'enjoy' on?"

💡 Teaching Tip

Encourage students to self-correct. When they make a gerund/infinitive error during the lesson, ask: "Gerund or infinitive?" — don't just correct it for them.
